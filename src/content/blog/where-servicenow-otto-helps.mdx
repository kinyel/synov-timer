ServiceNow Otto is the new name for ServiceNow's AI experience. Announced in May 2026, it brings Now Assist, Moveworks and AI Experience together, and ServiceNow is replacing the Now Assist name with Otto across its products. Existing licences carry over.

Whatever it is called, the question we hear most is the same: will it actually help us? In our experience the answer depends less on the AI and more on what it is given to work with.

## Where it helps

**Summaries.** Incident, chat and change summaries save agents from reading long threads before they can act. This is useful from day one, because it only needs the record itself.

**Drafting from the fix.** Otto can draft resolution notes and turn a resolved incident into a knowledge article. Agents review and publish rather than write from scratch.

**Answers before tickets.** In the portal, Teams or Slack, conversational self-service can answer common questions from your knowledge base and open the right request when it cannot.

**Triage.** AI agents for ITSM can set the category, the affected configuration item and the assignment group, and link an incident to a related major incident or problem.

**Building.** For developers, Otto for Creator and Build Agent generate code, flows and tests from plain language, inside ServiceNow Studio and common AI coding tools.

## Where it disappoints

**A thin or outdated knowledge base.** Conversational answers are only as good as the articles behind them. If your knowledge base is sparse, contradictory or years old, the AI will confidently repeat it.

**A CMDB nobody trusts.** If the configuration data is wrong, an agent that sets the affected item will set the wrong one, faster.

**Processes nobody agrees on.** AI can follow a process. It cannot settle an argument about what the process should be.

**No governance.** Without clear permissions and an audit trail, AI either does too little because people are nervous, or too much without anyone being able to show what it did. AI Control Tower exists for this.

**No baseline.** If you do not measure handle times or self-service rates before you start, you will not be able to show value afterwards.

## A short readiness check

Before switching anything on, we look at five things:

1. **Release and licences:** is the instance on a supported release, with the right entitlements?
2. **Knowledge:** coverage, quality and ownership of articles for your most common requests.
3. **Configuration data:** CMDB health for the classes the AI will use.
4. **Process:** agreed routing, categories and assignment groups.
5. **Governance and measures:** who approves AI use cases, and which numbers you will track.

## How we approach it

We start narrow: one or two use cases where the data is ready, measured against a baseline. When they work, we expand, adding custom skills built with the AI Skill Kit where the out-of-the-box skills do not fit, and connecting outside agents through MCP where it helps.
